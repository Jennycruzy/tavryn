# Value

## 1. The problem in one sentence

**Nigerian small businesses that supply large companies** struggle to **get cash against invoices those companies have agreed to pay** because **no lender can see whether the same invoice is already financed at another lender**, which costs them **30 to 60 days of waiting, or property and guarantees they often don't have, while lenders turn good businesses away.**

## 2. The value you create

- **Value proposition in one line:** Every invoice, financed once, so lenders can say yes to the invoice instead of asking for property.
- **Why users would switch:** Lenders get the one fact they're missing ("does somebody already have a claim on this invoice?") without exposing their book. Suppliers get competing offers instead of a paperwork wall. Buyers keep their prices private and pay nothing per check.

## 3. Why it matters

- **Cost of the problem:** Unmet demand for credit by Nigerian MSMEs is about ₦13 trillion (US$32.2bn) (IFC). When double financing does happen, the losses are large: First Brands (US, 2025) collapsed owing US$2.3bn to invoice financiers, and is being investigated for receivables factored more than once.
- **How many have it:** Fewer than 1 in 20 Nigerian MSMEs have access to bank credit (World Bank, Dec 2025).
- **Evidence:**
  - SME lending officer, Nigerian commercial bank: "There is no central place where we can check that in Nigeria." And: "A small business may have a genuine ₦20 million invoice from a strong company but no property to give as collateral. You can still end up saying no."
  - Building-material supplier: "If I already have to bring property before you give me money, then what is the point of the invoice?"
  - Accounts manager, large construction company: "We cannot tell them whether that supplier has already shown the same invoice to another lender."
  - Five consented interviews (two buyers, a bank lender, two suppliers), all agreeing that nobody will share prices in a registry, that an "already used" check is welcome, and that the lender should pay for it.

## 4. Why now

- **What changed:** Supplier finance is arriving in Nigeria: CycleFlow (C2FO with the IFC) launched there in 2026, and the World Bank approved US$500m for Nigerian small-business finance in December 2025. More lenders entering means more chances to finance the same invoice twice. Canton now has a live DevNet with Canton Coin, so the check and the payment can run on shared infrastructure.
- **Why this couldn't be solved well before:** Nigeria's National Collateral Registry lets lenders register claims on receivables, but lenders still prefer property, and the IFC and World Bank recommend wider use of it. It records that a claim exists. It cannot let two lenders race for one invoice and guarantee that exactly one wins, in real time, while keeping each lender's deal private.

## 5. Why Canton

- **What Canton makes possible here:** Privacy between parties: each lender, the buyer and the supplier see only their own contracts. A multi-party workflow: buyer approval, private offers and funding across separate companies. One-use shared state: when one lender funds, Canton consumes the invoice's funding right and refuses the rest (`LOCAL_VERDICT_LOCKED_CONTRACTS`, 45 of 45 races). Shared rules: the operators change them only with two of three votes.
- **Why a public chain or a plain database wouldn't do:** A public chain would expose every lender's clients and prices, which both buyers and the lender refused outright. A database needs one operator who sees everything, and no bank will hand its book to a competitor or a start-up.
