PROJECT NAME
Tavryn

TRACK
RWA & Business Workflows

CHALLENGE
BitSafe Challenge (Contribution Pool: Decentralizing Apps on Canton)

TAGLINE
Every invoice, financed once.

ELEVATOR PITCH
Tavryn is invoice finance for Nigerian suppliers. It stops the same invoice from being financed twice. Suppliers offer an approved invoice to several lenders in private; the first lender to pay wins, and everyone else is simply told it's taken, never who won, never at what price. The network itself refuses a second payment, so lenders get the guarantee without showing each other their books.

THE PROBLEM
Fewer than one in twenty Nigerian small businesses can get bank credit (World Bank, Dec 2025); their unmet demand is about N13 trillion (IFC). Many hold an invoice a large company has agreed to pay, and banks offer invoice discounting, but each bank sees only its own deals and cannot tell whether the invoice is already financed elsewhere. So banks demand guarantees and redirected buyer payments, and lend to few. A shared list of invoices would fix it, but would expose every lender's clients and prices.

WHY NOW
Supplier finance is arriving in Nigeria (CycleFlow, backed by C2FO and the IFC, launched in 2026), and the National Collateral Registry is still underused (IFC/World Bank, 2023). When the same receivable is financed twice, the losses are large: in September 2025 First Brands collapsed owing US$2.3bn to invoice financiers; its restructuring chief is investigating whether the same receivables were factored more than once (Global Trade Review, 30 Sep 2025). The same month, US prosecutors charged Tricolor's executives over double-pledging collateral to multiple lenders. Global factoring passed EUR 4 trillion a year in 2025 (FCI).

THE SOLUTION
Lenders compete privately for a buyer-approved invoice, and the Canton network accepts exactly one payment for it.

WHY CANTON
Canton lets lenders share one fact (whether an invoice is still available) without sharing their clients, prices or deals.

HOW IT WORKS
1. The supplier creates an invoice; only the supplier and buyer see it.
2. The buyer approves it. An invoice number can only be approved once.
3. The supplier offers it privately to several lenders.
4. The first lender to pay wins; the others are told it's no longer available.
5. The buyer repays the winner on the due date.

WHAT THE LOSING LENDER SEES
Its own offer, and: "This invoice is no longer available."

WHAT THE LOSING LENDER NEVER SEES
Who won, the amount, the price, or any other lender's business.

DECENTRALIZED BY DESIGN (BITSAFE)
The network's rules (which lenders may join, and how much of an invoice they may advance) are signed by all of the network's admins. A change needs two of three to agree; one admin alone is refused.

MEASURED
45 of 45 simultaneous payment races on LocalNet and the HackCanton DevNet: exactly one winner, every second lender refused by the network itself. Losing lender told in 0.75 s (median, LocalNet). Every duplicate approval refused. Canton Coin funding and repayment 5 of 5.

WORKING TODAY
- Live on the HackCanton DevNet: two lenders paid for the same invoice at the same moment; the network accepted one and refused the other. Rule changes were refused with one approval and applied with two.
- Real Canton Coin payments and repayments, with interrupted payments finished automatically and repayments never sent twice.
- A naira invoice on the HackCanton DevNet: two lenders raced for a ₦20.25m Lagos invoice, one funded it, the buyer repaid, and each bank payment reference is on the ledger.
- Canton Coin on the HackCanton DevNet: a lender paid a supplier 80 CC from its own DevNet wallet through Tavryn, and the rival lender was refused.
- A web app with a page for each company, and a recorded walkthrough of the full flow.

WHO USES IT
Large Nigerian buyers (manufacturers, FMCG, telecoms), their SME suppliers, and the banks, microfinance banks and fintech lenders that finance their invoices.

WHO PAYS
Lenders pay per invoice financed, because double financing is their loss.

CLOSING LINE
Every invoice, financed once. Prove it without revealing it.
