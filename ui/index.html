The problem

The same invoice gets sold twice.

A supplier waiting 60 days to be paid can sell its invoice to a lender for cash today. But each lender sees only its own deals — so one invoice can be financed by two or three lenders at once. The buyer pays once. Someone loses the money.

A shared list of invoices would stop it, but no lender will put its clients and prices where competitors can see them.

How it works

Four steps. One payment.

  1. Supplier creates the invoice

    Only the supplier and its buyer can see it.

  2. Buyer approves it — once

    An invoice number can never be approved a second time.

  3. Lenders get private offers

    Each lender sees only the offer made to it.

  4. First to pay wins

    The rest are told "no longer available". The buyer repays the winner.

Why it's safe

Guaranteed by the network, not by a promise.

One payment per invoice

When two lenders tried to pay for the same invoice at the same moment, the network accepted one and refused the other. No company database decides it.

Private by design

Each company sees only its own business. A lender that lost an invoice learns it's gone — nothing about who won or the price.

Real money, never paid twice

Lenders pay and buyers repay in Canton Coin. If a payment is interrupted, Tavryn finishes it automatically — a retry can never pay twice.

No single owner

Which lenders may join, and how much of an invoice they can advance, is set by the network's admins together. One admin alone can't change it.
